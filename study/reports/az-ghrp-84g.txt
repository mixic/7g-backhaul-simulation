What happened?
Between 04:24 on 29 May 2026 and 02:30 UTC on 30 May 2026, customers with resources in the West US 2 region may have experienced failures when attempting to access or manage Azure services in the region. Impact included connectivity failures, resource unavailability, and failures when attempting to create, update, or scale resources. The issue was triggered by a severe thunderstorm with lightning strikes that resulted in utility power loss to multiple datacenter facilities serving the region.
Backup generators activated as designed, however some datacenter areas experienced elevated temperatures and cooling-related issues. These conditions led to thermal-triggered protective shutdowns of infrastructure, affecting compute, networking, and storage systems. While power and cooling were restored within hours, storage systems required extended manual validation before they could safely be returned to service, which prolonged the total duration of customer impact.
The following services were among those affected: Application Insights, Azure App Service, Azure Backup, Azure Cache for Redis, Azure Container Registry, Azure Cosmos DB, Azure Databricks, Azure Data Explorer, Azure Data Factory, Azure Functions, Azure IoT Hub, Azure Kubernetes Service (AKS), Azure Monitor, Azure NetApp Files, Azure Resource Manager, Azure Site Recovery, Azure SQL Database, Azure Storage, Azure Synapse Analytics, Azure Virtual Machines, Log Analytics, Service Bus, and Virtual Machine Scale Sets – and potentially additional services, dependent on the affected infrastructure in the region.
What went wrong and why?
On 29 May 2026, a severe thunderstorm produced multiple lightning strikes, causing utility power loss to multiple datacenter facilities serving the West US 2 region simultaneously. These utility power issues affected a wide enough geographic area that they ultimately impacted datacenters in each of the three Availability Zones that comprise the region. In response, our backup generators activated as designed, and were able to take over the IT load. This transition to generator power worked successfully, across each of the Availability Zones, so the utility power disruption did not directly impact any IT infrastructure or customer workloads.
Independent of the transition from utility to generator power, a subset of the components within the mechanical cooling system faulted, resulting in them entering a 'lockout' protective state. This caused elevated temperatures as cooling systems were unable to maintain normal operating conditions. As temperatures rose, thermal protection systems automatically shut down infrastructure to prevent physical damage and protect data integrity. This resulted in loss of availability for services running on the affected infrastructure, which included at least one datacenter within each Availability Zone.
These cooling faults were driven by a systemic failure within the cooling infrastructure, which occurred in multiple Availability Zones and resulted in repeated failures, despite our design that generally isolates Availability Zones. These cooling issues required manual interventions, which took longer than expected to restore full cooling capacity as several components required troubleshooting and multiple restart attempts.
Our full investigation into the factors that contributed to this incident is ongoing. We are examining the specific conditions that led to elevated temperatures despite generator activation, and infrastructure improvements to reduce both the likelihood and recovery time of similar events.
How did we respond?
Recovery efforts began immediately - restoring cooling systems first, then bringing network infrastructure back online. Compute resources were largely recoverable through standard restart procedures. However, storage recovery required a more deliberate approach - affected storage systems needed manual data integrity validation before being returned to service. This validation is designed to ensure that customer data remains consistent and uncorrupted, but must be performed sequentially and cannot be significantly accelerated. Storage recovery was the primary factor in the extended duration of this incident. As storage systems came back online, dependent services recovered progressively. Services with the longest recovery times (including Application Insights and Log Analytics) had additional dependencies on the storage systems that were last to complete validation, followed by processing any backlogged telemetry and log data events that had accumulated.
04:14 UTC on 29 May 2026 – Severe weather caused utility power loss to multiple datacenter facilities in the region; automatic transfer to backup generators initiated and was successful.
04:24 UTC on 29 May 2026 – Customer impact began; environmental monitoring detected thermal alerts, as temperatures rose as a result of mechanical cooling system faults.
04:31 UTC on 29 May 2026 – Manual cooling diagnosis and restoration began, including resets of chillers and fluid coolers.
04:40 UTC on 29 May 2026 – As temperatures rose, thermal protection systems began automatically shutting down infrastructure to prevent damage.
04:50 UTC on 29 May 2026 – Networking and Storage engineers began assessing infrastructure health and necessary restoration efforts.

06:15 UTC on 29 May 2026 – Approximately 50% of impacted underlying Virtual Machines (VMs) had recovered, after temperatures had stabilized.
12:00 UTC on 29 May 2026 – Approximately 95% of impacted underlying Virtual Machines (VMs) had recovered, after premium Storage services had been largely recovered. Compute and Storage teams continued to identify and mitigate remaining unhealthy nodes.
18:46 UTC on 29 May 2026 – Storage was confirmed as the remaining recovery bottleneck; validation of Storage-specific issues were ongoing.
20:18 UTC on 29 May 2026 – All affected services confirmed as recovered, except for Application Insights and Log Analytics which were still processing backlogs.
02:30 UTC on 30 May 2026 – Customer impact to Log Analytics and Application Insights confirmed as mitigated.
How are we making incidents like this less likely or less impactful?
Cooling system recovery guidance: We are reviewing and enhancing our operational processes for cooling system recovery based on learnings from this incident, to ensure that all employees have clear and documented actions to support faster recovery during high-stress scenarios. (Estimated completion: June 2026)
Full system evaluation of cooling system protection logic: We are performing a joint engineering review to evaluate the fail-state behavior of the cooling system protection logic - the current system appears to prioritize equipment protection over operational continuity, which resulted in a full lockout rather than staged or degraded operation. (Estimated completion: October 2026)
We continue to investigate the ways in which services with long-tail recovery times (including Storage, Log Analytics, and Application Insights) can recover from scenarios like this one more quickly. (Estimated completion: TBD)

How can customers make incidents like this less impactful?
For mission-critical workloads, customers should consider a multi-region deployment strategy to maintain availability during events that affect a single region: https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/architecture/patterns/geodes and https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/well-architected/design-guides/regions-availability-zones
More generally, consider evaluating the reliability of your applications using guidance from the Azure Well-Architected Framework and its interactive Well-Architected Review: https://aka.ms/AzPIR/WAF
The impact times above represent the full incident duration, so are not specific to any individual customer. Actual impact to service availability varied between customers and resources – for guidance on implementing monitoring to understand granular impact: https://aka.ms/AzPIR/Monitoring
Finally, consider ensuring that the right people in your organization will be notified about any future service issues – by configuring Azure Service Health alerts. These can trigger emails, SMS, push notifications, webhooks, and more: https://aka.ms/AzPIR/Alerts
